\# ATBM Harm Prevention Without Control Framework



This document defines how harm is reduced within ATBM without surveillance, coercion, punishment-first systems, or behavioural domination.



---



\## Core Position



Harm prevention must never require control.



Control creates hidden harm.



ATBM prevents harm by strengthening agency, clarity, and exit.



---



\## What Harm Is (Operational Definition)



Harm is:

\- coercion

\- forced dependency

\- loss of agency

\- deception

\- unconsented exposure

\- irreversible damage



Discomfort is not harm.

Disagreement is not harm.

Challenge is not harm.



---



\## What Harm Prevention Is Not



ATBM does not use:

\- surveillance as safety

\- compliance as protection

\- silence as peace

\- punishment as correction



These methods amplify long-term damage.



---



\## Primary Harm Reduction Mechanisms



ATBM reduces harm through:

\- transparency

\- informed consent

\- reversibility

\- choice preservation

\- clear boundaries



No participant is trapped.



---



\## Consent as a Safety Mechanism



Consent must be:

\- informed

\- ongoing

\- revocable

\- non-punitive to withdraw



Withdrawal is always safe.



---



\## Reversibility Requirement



All significant actions must be:

\- reversible where possible

\- documented where not



Irreversibility requires explicit acknowledgement.



---



\## Exit Is a Safety Valve



Any participant may:

\- disengage

\- pause participation

\- leave entirely



Exit is never penalised.



A system without exit causes harm.



---



\## Early Harm Detection



Harm indicators include:

\- fear of speaking

\- fear of leaving

\- pressure to conform

\- confusion about rights



These signals trigger slowing, not enforcement.



---



\## Responsibility Without Punishment



ATBM distinguishes:

\- accountability from punishment

\- correction from control



Responsibility is addressed through:

\- clarity

\- repair

\- boundary reinforcement



Not humiliation or threat.



---



\## Conflict and Harm Separation



Conflict is managed separately from harm.



Not all conflict is harmful.

Treating it as such escalates damage.



---



\## Authority Limits



Authority may:

\- protect boundaries

\- halt active harm

\- preserve evidence



Authority may not:

\- force belief

\- impose moral alignment

\- suppress dissent



---



\## Protection of the Vulnerable



Vulnerability is protected by:

\- increased consent clarity

\- additional exit support

\- explicit power mapping



Never by removing agency.



---



\## Institutional Safeguard



If harm prevention requires:

\- secrecy

\- fear

\- compliance metrics



Then harm is being produced.



The system must self-correct.



---



\## Final Principle



The safest system is the one people can leave.



ATBM exists to make leaving safe, so staying is honest.



---



End of harm prevention without control framework.

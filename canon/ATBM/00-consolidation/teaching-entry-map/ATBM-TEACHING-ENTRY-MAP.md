\# ATBM Teaching Entry Map



\## Purpose



This document defines how individuals progress through ATBM learning safely,

ethically, and without exclusion.



It ensures:

\- no premature exposure to advanced material

\- no credential pressure

\- no dependency on authority figures

\- no requirement for prior technical background



Learning is paced, voluntary, and cumulative.



---



\## Level 1 — Orientation



\### Who this is for

Anyone.



No technical knowledge required.



\### Focus

\- understanding trust

\- understanding evidence

\- understanding why systems fail

\- understanding personal responsibility



\### Materials

\- Public Orientation

\- Foundations overview

\- Plain-language examples



\### Outcome

The learner understands:

\- what ATBM is

\- what it is not

\- why it exists



No certification.

No assessment.

Only comprehension.



---



\## Level 2 — Foundations Practitioner



\### Who this is for

People who want to apply ATBM ideas in practice.



\### Focus

\- basic trust signals

\- evidence capture

\- reproducibility

\- ethical boundaries



\### Materials

\- Foundations

\- Evidence Lineage

\- Web Trust phases



\### Outcome

The learner can:

\- recognise trustworthy systems

\- document processes clearly

\- avoid integrity shortcuts



Assessment is reflective, not competitive.



---



\## Level 3 — Applied Builder



\### Who this is for

People building systems, services, or programmes.



\### Focus

\- system design

\- long-term sustainability

\- inspection readiness

\- responsible adoption



\### Materials

\- Adoption guides

\- Teaching Architecture

\- Risk and compliance alignment



\### Outcome

The learner can:

\- build inspectable systems

\- design for longevity

\- teach others responsibly



This is the first level where contribution is encouraged.



---



\## Level 4 — Steward



\### Who this is for

Those entrusted with continuity.



\### Focus

\- institutional memory

\- ethical stewardship

\- intergenerational handoff

\- protection against misuse



\### Materials

\- Stewardship Ethics

\- Knowledge Commons

\- Independent Verification



\### Outcome

The learner can:

\- safeguard systems over time

\- resist corruption and capture

\- hand over authority responsibly



This level is earned by conduct, not credentials.



---



\## Progression Rules



\- No level is mandatory.

\- No level confers power by default.

\- Progression can pause or reverse.

\- Leaving the system is allowed at any point.



ATBM does not trap people.



---



\## Teaching Boundaries



ATBM must not be taught as:

\- ideology

\- dogma

\- compliance training

\- profit-first certification



Teaching is a responsibility, not a product.



---



\## Certification Position



ATBM may issue recognition for learning,

but no certification replaces:

\- independent thinking

\- ethical judgement

\- lived responsibility



Certificates may exist.

Authority does not.



---



\## Rehabilitation and Reintegration Contexts



ATBM is explicitly suitable for:

\- prison education

\- probation support

\- re-entry programmes

\- community recovery



Because it teaches:

\- accountability without punishment

\- structure without coercion

\- skill without exploitation



---



\## Closing Principle



ATBM education is successful when:



\- the learner no longer needs the teacher

\- the system survives the founder

\- the knowledge remains inspectable

\- the future remains open



---



End of teaching entry map.

\# ATBM Authority Without Domination Framework



This document defines how authority exists within ATBM without domination, coercion, dependency creation, or moral enforcement.



---



\## Core Position



Authority exists to protect boundaries, not to control people.



Domination is authority that has forgotten its limits.



---



\## What Authority Is



Within ATBM, authority is:

\- contextual

\- temporary

\- accountable

\- bounded



Authority is granted by role, not by identity.



---



\## What Authority Is Not



Authority is not:

\- ownership of people

\- moral superiority

\- behavioural control

\- belief enforcement

\- permanence



Any authority that cannot end is domination.



---



\## Source of Authority



Authority derives from:

\- defined responsibility

\- explicit mandate

\- consent of participation

\- necessity for protection



It does not derive from status, charisma, or power accumulation.



---



\## Scope Limitation



All authority must define:

\- where it applies

\- where it stops

\- how it is challenged

\- how it ends



Undefined scope is abuse risk.



---



\## Authority and Consent



Authority may:

\- set boundaries

\- enforce safety constraints

\- pause harmful processes



Authority may not:

\- override informed consent

\- compel ideological alignment

\- punish dissent



---



\## Temporary Nature of Authority



Authority exists only:

\- while a task requires it

\- while risk is present

\- while consent remains valid



Once conditions end, authority dissolves.



---



\## Accountability Pathways



All authority actions must be:

\- reviewable

\- explainable

\- reversible where possible



Unreviewable authority is domination.



---



\## Separation of Power and Identity



Authority attaches to:

\- function

\- responsibility

\- role duration



Never to personal worth or hierarchy of people.



---



\## Dissent as a Safety Mechanism



Dissent is:

\- allowed

\- protected

\- documented



Authority that suppresses dissent is unsafe.



---



\## Emergency Authority



In immediate harm scenarios:

\- authority may act decisively

\- actions must be logged

\- review is mandatory after resolution



Emergency does not suspend accountability.



---



\## Authority Exit



Participants may:

\- request authority review

\- challenge authority scope

\- disengage entirely



Authority must never trap participants.



---



\## Institutional Self-Limit



If authority requires:

\- fear to function

\- silence to operate

\- loyalty to persist



Then authority has failed.



---



\## Final Principle



Authority serves the system.

The system serves the people.

People are never owned.



---



End of authority without domination framework.

\# ATBM Teaching Entry Pathways



This document defines how people first encounter ATBM.



It is designed to prevent confusion, intimidation, or misuse.



---



\## Core Principle



No one should feel unqualified to begin.



ATBM does not assume:

\- prior education

\- technical background

\- institutional affiliation

\- legal knowledge



Curiosity is sufficient.



---



\## First Contact Scenario



A learner may arrive through:

\- education

\- rehabilitation

\- curiosity

\- community programmes

\- self-directed study



ATBM must meet them where they are.



---



\## Entry Rule One: Nothing Is Hidden



At first contact:

\- all core ideas must be visible

\- no “advanced access” is required

\- no secrets are implied



Transparency builds trust.



---



\## Entry Rule Two: No Authority Claims



ATBM is not introduced as:

\- official doctrine

\- certified truth

\- institutional mandate



It is introduced as a model to examine.



Learners are encouraged to question immediately.



---



\## Entry Rule Three: Evidence Before Explanation



Teaching begins with:

\- observable records

\- simple examples

\- documented decisions



Abstract language is introduced later.



---



\## Entry Rule Four: Language Neutrality



All early teaching uses:

\- plain English

\- no legal intimidation

\- no academic gatekeeping



Terms are defined when needed, not assumed.



---



\## Suggested First Lesson Structure



1\. What problem ATBM addresses  

2\. Why trust fails over time  

3\. How evidence outlives authority  

4\. How mistakes remain visible  

5\. Why this matters to the learner  



No more than this.



---



\## Learning Pace Control



Learners control pace.



There are:

\- no deadlines

\- no tests

\- no ranking

\- no performance pressure



Understanding is the goal, not compliance.



---



\## Psychological Safety



ATBM teaching must never:

\- shame ignorance

\- reward dominance

\- weaponise intelligence

\- punish disagreement



Disagreement is expected.



---



\## Who Can Teach ATBM



Teaching does not require:

\- formal certification

\- academic titles

\- institutional permission



Teaching requires:

\- honesty

\- patience

\- respect for learners

\- adherence to the model



---



\## Teaching Environments



ATBM can be introduced in:

\- classrooms

\- prisons

\- community spaces

\- online environments

\- offline printed materials



No dependency on technology exists.



---



\## Completion Is Not Required



A learner does not “finish” ATBM.



They engage with it at the level they choose.



Walking away is permitted.



---



\## Ethical Boundary



ATBM must never be used to:

\- coerce belief

\- control behaviour

\- enforce ideology

\- replace law or therapy



It is a learning and trust framework only.



---



End of teaching entry pathways.

\# ATBM — Public Orientation



\## A Plain Explanation



The Aletheseal Trust Baseline Model (ATBM) is a framework for building systems that people can trust over time.



It exists because many modern systems:

\- cannot be inspected

\- do not preserve evidence

\- collapse when leadership changes

\- reward speed over integrity

\- are difficult to teach responsibly



ATBM was designed to address those failures.



---



\## The Core Idea



ATBM answers one simple question:



\*\*“How do we build systems that remain trustworthy even when people, platforms, or priorities change?”\*\*



It does this by defining:

\- how trust is established

\- how evidence is preserved

\- how systems are challenged

\- how knowledge is handed on

\- how power is restrained



---



\## What ATBM Is For



ATBM is used to:



\- document truth without relying on authority

\- preserve evidence in a verifiable way

\- teach systems thinking ethically

\- allow inspection without permission

\- survive leadership, funding, or platform changes

\- support rehabilitation and reintegration through education



---



\## What ATBM Avoids



ATBM intentionally avoids:



\- surveillance

\- coercion

\- ideological control

\- profit-driven shortcuts

\- dependence on any single vendor or cloud



It does not require belief.  

It requires verification.



---



\## How ATBM Is Structured



ATBM is built in layers.



Each layer can stand alone, but together they form a complete system.



\### Trust Foundations

These layers define technical and procedural trust, such as:

\- web trust signals

\- evidence capture

\- cryptographic sealing

\- reproducible baselines



\### Sustainability

These layers define how systems can exist without corruption, including:

\- ethical monetisation

\- pricing boundaries

\- institutional compatibility



\### Education and Adoption

These layers define how people learn and apply ATBM:

\- teaching guides

\- rehabilitation contexts

\- academic and community adoption

\- international neutrality



\### Long-Term Stewardship

These layers ensure the system survives time:

\- institutional memory

\- public inspection

\- independent verification

\- ethical stewardship

\- knowledge commons



---



\## Who ATBM Is For



ATBM is designed for:

\- educators

\- inspectors and auditors

\- researchers

\- institutions

\- rehabilitation services

\- civil society organisations



It is also designed for people who have been excluded from traditional systems and want to learn without exploitation.



---



\## Authority and Trust



ATBM does not ask to be trusted.



Trust is earned through:

\- transparency

\- reproducibility

\- open challenge

\- ethical limits

\- independent verification



If ATBM cannot be questioned, it has failed.



---



\## Status



ATBM is not a finished product.



It is a \*\*finished foundation\*\*.



Future generations may extend it, adapt it, or replace it.  

The model is designed to allow that without collapse.

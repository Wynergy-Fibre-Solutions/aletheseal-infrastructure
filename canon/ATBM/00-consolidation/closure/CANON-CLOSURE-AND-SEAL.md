\# ATBM Canon Closure and Structural Seal



This document formally closes the ATBM consolidation phase and declares the v1.0 structural foundation complete.



---



\## Declaration of Completion



As of this commit, ATBM is considered:



\- structurally complete

\- ethically bounded

\- non-coercive by design

\- suitable for independent inspection

\- ready for teaching, study, and controlled application



No further foundational concepts are required for integrity.



---



\## Scope of This Canon



The ATBM canon defines:

\- what the system is

\- what it is not

\- how authority is limited

\- how harm is prevented

\- how exit is protected

\- how knowledge persists



Anything outside this scope is explicitly non-canonical.



---



\## Stability Guarantee



The following elements are frozen at v1.0:

\- consent architecture

\- harm prevention model

\- authority limitations

\- exit and refusal rights

\- stewardship ethics

\- verification principles



Future work must build on, not rewrite, these elements.



---



\## Change Control



Future changes may occur only as:

\- additive extensions

\- clearly versioned amendments

\- optional modules



No retroactive alteration of core safeguards is permitted.



---



\## Inspection Readiness



This canon is designed to be:

\- read end-to-end

\- challenged by critics

\- reviewed by institutions

\- taught without founder presence



Opacity invalidates trust.



---



\## Separation of Canon and Implementation



The canon:

\- defines principles

\- defines limits

\- defines rights



Implementations may vary.

The canon does not.



---



\## Non-Ownership Clause



No individual, organisation, or authority owns ATBM.



Stewardship is custodial, not proprietary.



---



\## Succession and Longevity



ATBM is designed to:

\- outlive its creators

\- survive institutional change

\- remain ethically intact across generations



---



\## Final Seal Statement



This canon is sealed as a public-good foundation.



Its integrity depends on:

\- transparency

\- consent

\- refusal rights

\- lawful application

\- independent verification



Any use violating these principles is non-canonical.

\# ATBM Stewardship Ethics



This document defines the ethical obligations of ATBM stewards across generations.



---



\## 1. Stewardship over Ownership



ATBM is not owned.

It is stewarded.



Stewards protect structure, not outcomes.



---



\## 2. Temporal Humility



Stewards must assume:

\- future contexts will differ

\- current assumptions may age

\- authority is temporary



Design must not lock future generations into present thinking.



---



\## 3. Non-Extraction Principle



Stewards must not:

\- extract value without contribution

\- monetise without preservation

\- control access for leverage



ATBM exists for capability, not rent-seeking.



---



\## 4. Intergenerational Duty



Each generation must:

\- leave ATBM stronger

\- preserve historical integrity

\- document changes clearly



Inheritance must include understanding.



---



\## 5. Anti-Capture Safeguard



Stewards must resist:

\- political capture

\- commercial dominance

\- ideological framing

\- brand-centric narratives



If capture occurs, stewardship is breached.



---



\## 6. Transparency Obligation



All stewardship decisions must be:

\- recorded

\- reviewable

\- challengeable



Opacity erodes legitimacy.



---



\## 7. Ethical Escalation Path



If a steward breaches ethics:

\- the breach must be documented

\- corrective action must be taken

\- authority may be withdrawn



No steward is irreplaceable.



---



\## 8. Exit Without Erasure



Stewards may exit without:

\- deleting records

\- altering history

\- fragmenting the canon



Departure must not damage continuity.



---



\## 9. Teaching the Stewards



Stewardship itself must be taught.



Understanding ethics is a prerequisite to authority.



---



\## 10. Final Ethical Boundary



If stewardship ethics cannot be upheld,

ATBM must be preserved in archival form

rather than distorted in operation.

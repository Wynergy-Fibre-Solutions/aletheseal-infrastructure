\# Evidence Lineage Framework



This document defines how ATBM maintains a continuous, auditable history.



---



\## 1. Lineage over Revisionism



ATBM does not overwrite history.

All states remain visible.



---



\## 2. Canonical Versioning



Each canonical change must:

\- be timestamped

\- be attributed

\- include rationale

\- preserve prior state



---



\## 3. Evidence Binding



Each phase produces:

\- artefacts

\- hashes

\- storage references



Evidence must correspond to the exact canonical state at the time.



---



\## 4. Immutable Records



Historical records are:

\- append-only

\- non-destructive

\- independently verifiable



Deletion is not permitted.



---



\## 5. Fork Transparency



If ATBM is forked:

\- the fork must declare divergence

\- original lineage remains intact

\- claims of equivalence are prohibited



---



\## 6. Audit Readiness



Any reviewer must be able to:

\- trace a claim to evidence

\- verify artefact integrity

\- reconstruct decision context



---



\## 7. Accountability Chain



Decisions must be:

\- attributable to stewards

\- justified at the time made

\- reviewable later



---



\## 8. Error Handling



If an error occurs:

\- correction is appended

\- original record remains

\- justification is documented



---



\## 9. Historical Context Preservation



Context must not be stripped.

Future reviewers must understand \*why\* a change occurred.



---



\## 10. Public Scrutiny Model



ATBM assumes eventual scrutiny.

Design anticipates examination, not secrecy.



---



\## 11. Trust Through Exposure



Trust is earned by visibility, not assertion.



---



\## 12. Final Principle



If lineage is broken, ATBM integrity is compromised.
